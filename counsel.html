Legal Insights

Know Your Rights

Land Law
Your Name Is Not on the Title. Does the Land Still Belong to You?
Under the Land Act (Cap 227), beneficial interest can exist without formal registration, particularly in customary and matrimonial contexts. Many Ugandans abandon valid land claims because the title does not carry their name. If you have occupied, improved, or paid for land over a sustained period, you may hold an enforceable claim. A legal opinion costs far less than surrendering land you own.
Commercial Law
Why Ugandan Businesses Lose Contracts and How to Stop It
The majority of commercial disputes in Ugandan courts arise from contracts that were unsigned, ambiguous on payment terms, or silent on remedies. A written contract is not a formality, it is the document a court will read when the relationship breaks down. Every agreement involving money, services, or property must state: what is exchanged, by when, under what conditions, and what the remedy is for non-performance.
Family Law
Child Custody in Uganda: What the Court Actually Considers
Ugandan courts determine custody on the best interests of the child, not the rights of parents. Factors include age, established relationships, stability, financial capacity, and in older children, the child's own preference. Mothers are not automatically favoured, nor are fathers excluded. The outcome depends heavily on how each parent's situation is presented in court. Preparation at this stage is not optional, orders made are legally binding and difficult to vary.
